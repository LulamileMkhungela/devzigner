Domain experts: Now is your moment

If you know a hard problem from the inside, you're not behind on building. You're way ahead.

July 7, 2026Brian Casel

If you've ever decided not to build something because you can't code, I want to change your mind.

"Coding" was never the hard part.

The hard part was always knowing the right problem to solve and the right solution to build. Writing the code was just the thing you had to learn or hire out before you could ship. So plenty of people who knew exactly what their industry needed never built it, because they couldn't get past the code.

Here's what changed: the building is learnable now. You don't have to get past it anymore.

You're holding the part that's actually scarce

Think about what you have if you know a hard problem from the inside.

You know your problem-space better than 99% of the world. You know the workflow everyone pretends is fine and quietly hates. You know the one step that breaks every time, the data that never lines up, the thing every off-the-shelf tool gets wrong because whoever built it had never done the job.

Call it insider info on a hard problem. That's the part AI can't do for you. It can't do it for a pro developer either.

That knowledge used to be necessary but not enough. You also had to code, or pay someone who could and then spend months explaining your world to them. So the knowledge sat there, stuck behind the code you couldn't write.

Now that's gone, and the building is learnable. What you're left holding is the part that was always scarce.

The claim that's going to annoy a lot of developers

Here's where I'll lose some people.

I think a domain expert who can't write a line of code is now more qualified to build the tools for their field than a pro developer is.

The developer can build anything, but they don't know what to build. They'd have to learn your field for years to feel the problems you already feel in your bones. The rare, lucky case is the person who has both: insider knowledge of a niche and a software background. There just aren't many of them. There are vastly more people who have the inside knowledge and no code.

Picture a bookkeeper who's spent fifteen years inside the same messy month-end close. Hand that problem to a great developer and they'll build something clean and wrong, because they don't know which three numbers actually matter or the one reconciliation everyone fudges. Hand a capable AI to the bookkeeper and they'll build the thing that fits, because they've lived every edge case the tool has to handle. The code was never what stood between them and that tool. The inside knowledge was.

You're not behind. You're way ahead.

The exact thing that used to disqualify non-technical founders, "I can't code," is the thing AI just neutralized. And the exact thing you have that can't be shortcut, insider knowledge of a real problem, is now the scarce, decisive input.

The market spent twenty years telling these people they were on the outside of building. That was true when you needed code to build. It isn't true anymore. The people who were counted out are the ones holding the rare card now.

So you're not behind. With the one piece that can't be automated already in your pocket, you're way ahead.

This is your moment

If you've been telling yourself you'll build it once you learn to code, stop waiting. That was never the gap, and the building is learnable now anyway.

"Coding" was never the hard part. Now it isn't even your part to carry. The part that's left, knowing the right problem and the right solution, is the part you already have and the part nobody can hand you.

This is your moment to build. Not the developers'. Yours.
